# Cover Letter & Outreach Style Guide

## Cover letter structure

Three paragraphs, no more:

1. **Opening** — one declarative sentence stating who the candidate is and the role they're
   applying for. No rhetorical questions. Lead with whichever credential from their resume
   most directly matches the role's core requirement — this varies by JD, so don't default
   to just their most recent job; scan the whole work history for the strongest match.
2. **Evidence** — 2-3 concrete accomplishments mapped directly to the JD's stated
   requirements, drawn only from what's actually in the resume. Specific numbers and
   outcomes where the resume has them, not adjectives standing in for evidence.
3. **Close** — if the fit assessment surfaced a genuine gap, name it directly rather than
   omitting or hand-waving it, then close with a brief statement of interest. If there's no
   real gap, this paragraph can just reinforce fit and close.

## Formatting rules

- Clean, standard formatting: a widely-supported font (Calibri, Arial, or similar), no
  date line, justified or left-aligned body paragraphs, contact info at the top in the
  main document body (not a header/footer — see the ATS formatting checklist for why).
- Oxford commas throughout.
- No em dashes in the generated prose.
- Tight, declarative, first person. Specific and concrete, no filler adjectives.
- Shorter is better — if a sentence can be cut without losing information, cut it.
- Match the visual style of the candidate's actual resume where reasonable (same font
  family, similar header treatment) so the cover letter and resume read as a matched set.

## LinkedIn InMail / outreach note to hiring manager or recruiter

- 3-5 sentences, casual-professional register — InMail gets skimmed on mobile, so
  front-load the point.
- Open with the specific role and why the candidate is reaching out directly.
- One or two of their strongest, most relevant credentials — pulled from whichever part of
  their work history is the best match for this specific JD, not just their current job.
- Close with a low-friction ask: happy to share more, open to a quick conversation. No hard
  sell, no manufactured urgency.
- No em dashes, no rhetorical questions, no exclamation-point stacking.

## Both documents

- Every factual claim must trace back to something actually in the parsed resume text. If
  the ideal supporting detail isn't there, don't invent one — use the closest true detail
  instead, or drop the claim. Never round up years of experience, invent a metric, or
  imply ownership of something the resume only shows partial involvement in.
- Don't inflate a title, don't claim scope the resume doesn't support, and don't quietly
  drop qualifiers the resume includes (e.g. "informed" GTM strategy becoming "led" GTM
  strategy).
